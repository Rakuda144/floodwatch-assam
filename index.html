About Assam Flood Monitor

👋 Who built this & why?

Hi, I'm Bijay from Charaideo, Assam. I'm not a flood expert or a hydrologist — just someone from Assam who built this site hoping that people across our state find it genuinely useful when the monsoon hits.

I was one of the many people who went through and suffered during the devastating Assam floods of 2026. When water rises quickly in the middle of the night, checking slow portals or waiting for scattered updates is stressful. I wanted a single, fast, mobile-friendly place where anyone — even on weak mobile internet — can quickly check nearby river gauges, see upstream water levels, and share clear updates with family and neighbours on WhatsApp.

📡 Where does the data come from?

Central Water Commission (CWC)

All river water levels, Warning Levels (WL), Danger Levels (DL), and Highest Flood Levels (HFL) are fetched from the official CWC Flood Forecasting System (FFS). Updated every few hours. CWC posts many gauges once a day, and each reading shows its own time.

Open-Meteo & Windy Radar

Hourly precipitation, 24-hour town rainfall estimates, and 7-day forecasts are powered by Open-Meteo, paired with Windy live cloud & rain radar for tracking storms over Assam and the upstream hills.

Map Tiles & Location Privacy

Map views use public Esri basemaps. If you tap Use My Location, your coordinates are sent only to api.bigdatacloud.net to look up your town name and api.open-meteo.com for local weather, and stored only on your device.

Emergency Helplines

Quick-dial numbers for ASDMA (1070), District DEOC (1077), ASDMA SEOC, 108, and NDRF are built directly into the SOS tab for one-tap calling.

Important Note & Disclaimer

Updated every few hours. CWC posts many gauges once a day, and each reading shows its own time so you always know how fresh a measurement is.

Assam Flood Monitor is an independent, non-commercial community tool and is not affiliated with any government agency. Always follow official advisories and evacuation orders from ASDMA, CWC, and your local District Administration.

1. Before Flood Waters Rise
  • Keep Aadhaar, land records, and medical prescriptions inside a sealed waterproof pouch.
  • Charge phones & power banks; keep torches, ORS packets, dry food, and drinking water ready.
  • Move cattle, goats, and vehicles to higher ground early when upstream gauges begin rising.
2. During Flooding
  • Turn off the main electrical breaker and gas regulator if water enters your courtyard or home.
  • Never walk, swim, or drive across fast-flowing floodwater or submerged culverts.
  • Stay alert for snakes and snapped power lines around flooded compounds at night.
3. After Water Recedes
  • Boil or chlorinate all drinking water before use to prevent waterborne illnesses.
  • Inspect damp electrical switches and wiring before switching mains power back on.
  • Report embankment breaches or stranded families to District DEOC 1077.
